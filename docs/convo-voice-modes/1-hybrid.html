What to look at

  • Mode first, cause above effect. The mode card sits above the three cards it controls, so when a later mode hides or locks a card the reason is on screen above it.
  • Four fixed questions. “Who does what” answers the same four questions in every mode: who hears you, who decides when to answer, who speaks, who approves actions. Comparing modes means comparing four lines.
  • Plain names. Each mode has a one-line explanation. “Duplex” never appears on the card; Native is described as “listens and talks at once” and the word is explained in the design doc only.
  • Not built ≠ hidden. Native and Tuned are listed but disabled, with the reason as visible text tied to the radio by aria-describedby (never a tooltip), plus the same “Why some … are not available” disclosure the provider select already uses.
  • Evidence per claim. “What Convo knows” is a table: each claim has its own source and date, so a checked fact and a vendor claim are never merged into one badge.

Keyboard

  • Native radio group: Tab enters at the chosen mode, arrow keys move between the available ones, disabled modes are skipped. Nothing saves until Save (the shared draft).
  • The disclosures are <details>: Enter or Space opens them.
